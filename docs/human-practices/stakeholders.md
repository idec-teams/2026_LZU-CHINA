# Stakeholder dialogue: beyond the initial idea

<p class="page-deck">Read how healthcare and industry perspectives introduced practical constraints into the project conversation.</p>

Our Entrepreneurship and Inclusivity records describe conversations with healthcare and industry stakeholders. Here we carry forward the community engagement lessons: understanding practical needs, examining assumptions and recognising what responsible translation would require.

## Healthcare perspectives

ICU nursing staff highlighted sample-handling burdens and the difficulty of reading colour signals under dim ward lighting. Grassroots laboratory staff emphasised concise procedures and reduced dependence on large instruments. Representatives of the Gansu Red Cross Society drew attention to people who cannot rely on visual readouts.

These discussions informed prospective priorities: simpler workflows, clearer interpretation, complementary non-visual communication and attention to affordability. The inclusivity work gathered patient-oriented perspectives through professionals and representatives, rather than direct interviews with critically ill patients. See the [full dialogue and resource record](inclusivity.md).

## Industry perspectives

### Jinghong Health Products Ltd.

Our conversation with Mr. Liu Jinghong, Founder and General Manager, broadened the discussion from a scientific concept to the elements of a usable product. Formulation, product presentation, communication, intellectual property and coordination across teams all entered the conversation. The lesson for our planning was to connect scientific credibility with a coherent development process.

## Turning dialogue into questions for iDEC

| Lesson from the conversations | Question for future directed-evolution planning |
| --- | --- |
| A useful output must be understandable in its setting | Does the chosen measurement reflect a property that users can interpret? |
| Operating conditions vary across settings | Under which conditions must an improvement remain reliable? |
| Production consistency matters | How would reproducibility and stability be assessed beyond an initial screen? |
| Access and cost influence practical use | What burdens would the proposed workflow place on people and resources? |
| Communication must match evidence | Can we explain the baseline, improvement and limitations without overstating readiness? |

These are our iDEC reflections on the documented conversations. They describe future planning questions, not experiments or product performance established by the interviews.
